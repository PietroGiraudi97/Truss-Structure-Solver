Pin-Jointed Truss Analysis by the Unit-Load (Virtual Work) Method

This document describes exactly what the program computes, the assumptions made, and the mathematics behind the solver. The implementation follows this formulation line by line (see js/solver.js).

1. Problem statement & assumptions

A pin-jointed truss is a structure of straight bars connected at frictionless pins (joints). Loads are applied only at the joints. Under these conditions every member carries a constant axial force — there is no bending or shear — and each member behaves as a linear spring of stiffness EA/L.

The software assumes:

  • 2D (planar) geometry; joints carry two translational degrees of freedom (u_x, u_y), rotations are free at pins;
  • linear-elastic material, constant E and A per member;
  • small displacements (first-order theory, geometry taken from the undeformed state);
  • loads at joints only; distributed effects (self weight, temperature) are converted to equivalent joint data as detailed in §6–7;
  • supports: pin (both translations fixed) or rollers (one translation fixed).

2. Notation and member kinematics

Joint i has coordinates (x_i, y_i) [m] and displacement \mathbf{d}_i = (u_i, v_i) [mm]. For member m connecting joints n_1 \to n_2, let

L_m = \sqrt{(x_{n_2}-x_{n_1})^2 + (y_{n_2}-y_{n_1})^2}, \qquad (c_x, c_y) = \frac{(x_{n_2}-x_{n_1},\, y_{n_2}-y_{n_1})}{L_m}

be its length and direction cosines. The axial total elongation is the projection of the relative joint displacement onto the member axis:

\Delta L_m \;=\; \mathbf{b}_m^{\mathsf T}\,\mathbf{u}, \qquad \mathbf{b}_m = \begin{pmatrix} -c_x \\ -c_y \\ c_x \\ c_y \end{pmatrix}_{n_1x,\,n_1y,\,n_2x,\,n_2y}

3. Governing equations of the discrete truss

3.1 Constitutive law (including thermal strain)

With free thermal strain \varepsilon_t = \alpha\,\Delta T, the member force depends only on the mechanical strain:

N_m \;=\; \frac{E_m A_m}{L_m}\,\Big(\Delta L_m - \Delta L_m^{th}\Big), \qquad \Delta L_m^{th} = \alpha\,\Delta T_m\, L_m \qquad\text{(+ tension)}

3.2 Joint equilibrium

At every free degree of freedom k, member end forces must balance the external load P_k (applied loads plus the self-weight and thermal equivalent joint loads of §6–7):

\sum_m (\mathbf{b}_m)_k\, N_m \;=\; P_k

3.3 The assembled stiffness system

Substituting §3.1 into §3.2 and writing K = \sum_m \dfrac{E_mA_m}{L_m}\,\mathbf{b}_m\mathbf{b}_m^{\mathsf T}, the displacement vector of all free dofs satisfies

\mathbf{K}\,\mathbf{u} \;=\; \mathbf{P} \;+\; \sum_m \mathbf{b}_m\, F_{T,m}, \qquad F_{T,m} = E_m A_m \alpha\,\Delta T_m

where F_T is the thermal fixed-end force: the force a member fully restrained against expansion would carry (N = -F_T when \Delta L=0). This linear system is what the program solves first (Gaussian elimination with partial pivoting) to obtain the compatibility-consistent displacement field — the reference solution, exact for determinate and indeterminate trusses alike.

4. The unit-load (virtual work) theorem for displacements

The headline method. To find the displacement \delta_k of joint coordinate k:

  1. remove the real loads;
  2. apply a unit virtual load \bar{P}_k = 1 at coordinate k, in the desired direction;
  3. compute any force system n_m in the members that is in static equilibrium with that unit load.

Then the principle of virtual work — external virtual work equals internal virtual work — gives, exactly,

\boxed{\;\delta_k \;=\; \sum_m n_m\,\Delta L_m \;=\; \sum_m n_m\left(\frac{N_m L_m}{E_m A_m} \;+\; \alpha\,\Delta T_m L_m\right)\;}

because the real elongation field \Delta L_m (elastic + thermal), treated as the compatible "strain" the virtual system works through, is known from §3.

In the program this integration is carried out independently for every free dof, and the result is compared against the direct stiffness solution \mathbf{u}; agreement to machine precision is reported in the results panel ("Virtual-work check").

5. The redundant (statically indeterminate) case

Counting members m, reaction components r and joints j:

  • m + r < 2j — mechanism (unstable): singular K, the solver stops with an error;
  • m + r = 2j — statically determinate: equilibrium alone fixes all member forces;
  • m + r > 2j — indeterminate of degree m+r-2j.

In the indeterminate case the real forces N_m cannot be found from equilibrium alone; they additionally require compatibility, which is precisely what solving K\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{P} provides (§3.3).

Why the virtual work sum is still exact. The virtual n-forces are required to satisfy equilibrium only — not compatibility. If \mathbf{A}_{free}\,\mathbf{n} = \mathbf{e}_k has infinitely many solutions, any two differ by a self-stress state \mathbf{s} (\mathbf{A}\mathbf{s} = 0), and a self-stress does zero work on any compatible elongation field: \mathbf{s}^{\mathsf T}\Delta\mathbf{L} = \mathbf{s}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{B}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{u} = 0. Hence \sum n_m\Delta L_m is invariant, and the program simply picks the minimum-norm solution \mathbf{n} = \mathbf{A}^{\mathsf T}(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{A}^{\mathsf T})^{-1}\mathbf{e}_k (function Lin.solveMinNorm).

6. Self weight

Member weight w_m = \rho\, g\, A_m\, L_m ( \rho configurable, default steel 7850 kg/m³) acts along the member. As a prismatic pin-ended bar is statically equivalent to half its weight applied at each end joint, the program adds

P^{sw}_{n_1,y} = P^{sw}_{n_2,y} = -\,\frac{w_m}{2}

to the load vector. For the truss idealisation this lumping is exact: members carry pure axial force either way, and external work of the lumped system equals that of the distributed load. Weight is added only at solve time (not stored in the model), and the total is reported in the results panel. The classical single hanging bar is recovered: \delta_{tip} = wL/(2EA).

7. Thermal expansion

With the toggle on (\alpha default 12 µε/°C for steel), each member has a temperature change \Delta T_m (set individually, or globally with "Apply ΔT to all"). The constitutive law of §3.1 leads to the fixed-end-force formulation: a heated member pushes its joints apart with F_T = EA\alpha\Delta T, i.e. it acts like an equal opposite external load. Two canonical behaviours the tests in test.html verify:

  • Fully restrained member heated: \Delta L = 0, so N = -EA\alpha\Delta T (pure thermal compression);
  • Determinate truss with one expandable member: it lengthens freely (\Delta L = \alpha\Delta T L), joints move, and all member forces remain zero — temperature only induces stress where expansion is restrained (typical in redundant structures).

8. Reactions

Once all member forces are known, equilibrium of each supported joint gives the support forces on the structure (positive in +x, +y):

R_x = -P_x - \sum_{m \ni j} (\mathbf{b}_m)_x\, N_m, \qquad R_y = -P_y - \sum_{m \ni j} (\mathbf{b}_m)_y\, N_m

with the sums over members meeting at joint j, and the sign convention that \mathbf{b}_m points from the joint toward the far end. Global checks \sum R_x = -\sum P_x, \sum R_y = -\sum P_y hold identically and are exercised by the self-tests.

9. Stability and error detection

  • The status bar shows the counts j, m, r and warns when m+r < 2j (certain mechanism).
  • The true test is numerical: if K is singular (a rigid-body mode, or a local mechanism such as two collinear members at an unloaded joint), solving K\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{P} fails and the program reports the structure as unstable instead of producing numbers.
  • Zero-length members and fully-fixed structures are rejected with explanatory messages.

10. Numerical verification built into the program

Every solve reports two residuals in the results panel:

\text{equilibrium residual} \;=\; \big\|\mathbf{K}\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{P}\big\|_\infty, \qquad \text{virtual-work check} \;=\; \max_k \big| \delta_k - \textstyle\sum_m n_m \Delta L_m \big|

Both are at rounding level (~10^{-10}) for a well-posed structure. Additionally, test.html runs closed-form textbook cases (two-bar truss, method-of-joints bracket, hanging bar under self weight, restrained thermal bar, free thermal sway, Pratt truss with symmetry checks) and prints PASS/FAIL per identity.

12. Limitations

First-order linear theory only. Not covered: buckling of compression members (always check σ against the member's buckling capacity separately!), geometric nonlinearity / second-order (P–Δ) effects, plasticity, joint rigidity (real trusses have semi-rigid gussets), out-of-plane behaviour, and dynamic effects. The small-displacement assumption means members deform and forces are computed from the original geometry.

13. Dynamic analysis (modal superposition)

With inertia included, the equilibrium becomes the discretised equation of motion

\mathbf{M}\,\ddot{\mathbf{u}} + \mathbf{C}\,\dot{\mathbf{u}} + \mathbf{K}\,\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{P}(t)

13.1 Mass model

A lumped (diagonal) mass matrix is used: each member's mass \rho A_m L_m is split 50/50 to its two end joints, and arbitrary joint masses can be added per node. Consistent units are kN–mm–s with mass in \text{kt = Mg} (1\,\text{kN} = 1\,\text{kt}\cdot 1000\,\text{mm/s}^2). Degrees of freedom carrying no mass (e.g. a massless intermediate joint) are statically condensed out of the dynamic system.

13.2 Undamped free vibration — eigenpairs

\mathbf{K}\,\boldsymbol{\varphi}_r = \omega_r^2\,\mathbf{M}\,\boldsymbol{\varphi}_r, \qquad f_r = \frac{\omega_r}{2\pi}\;[\text{Hz}]

With diagonal M the generalised problem reduces to a standard symmetric one, (\mathbf{M}^{-1/2}\mathbf{K}\mathbf{M}^{-1/2})\,\boldsymbol{\psi} = \omega^2\boldsymbol{\psi}, solved by classical Jacobi rotations. Mode shapes are mass-normalised (\boldsymbol{\varphi}_r^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{M}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_r = 1) and can be animated on the structure.

13.3 Damped time response by modal superposition

Writing \mathbf{u}(t) = \sum_r \boldsymbol{\varphi}_r\, q_r(t) and using modal (Rayleigh) damping \zeta_r, every mode is an independent SDOF oscillator

\ddot{q}_r + 2\zeta_r\omega_r\dot{q}_r + \omega_r^2 q_r = \boldsymbol{\varphi}_r^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{P}\cdot\lambda(t)

with \lambda(t) the selectable load history (step, pulse, harmonic, ramp). Each modal equation is integrated exactly by Duhamel convolution with linear interpolation of the load within a step:

q_r(t+h) = e^{-\zeta\omega h}\big[A\cos\omega_d h + B\sin\omega_d h\big] + q_{p}(t+h), \qquad \omega_d = \omega\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}

where q_p(t) = \big(p(t) - \tfrac{2\zeta}{\omega}\dot p\big)/\omega^2 is the particular solution of the linearly-varying load. Member forces at every instant follow from N_m(t) = (EA/L)_m\,\mathbf{b}_m^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{u}(t), so the stress colour map on the deflected shape also animates. The results plot reports the dynamic amplification factor DAF = u_{max,dyn}/u_{static} (theory: exactly 2 for a sudden step load on an undamped SDOF — verified by test T8).

14. Design checks (utilisation & buckling)

For each member the program reports the utilisation

U_m = \frac{\sigma_m}{\sigma_{lim,m}}, \qquad \sigma_{lim} = \begin{cases} \sigma_{allow} & \text{tension}\\ \min(\sigma_{allow},\ \sigma_{cr}) & \text{compression}\end{cases}

where \sigma_{allow} is the user's allowable stress (material preset with a safety factor, e.g. S235 → 157 MPa = 235/1.5) and the Euler buckling stress of a pin-ended member is

\sigma_{cr} = \frac{\pi^2 E I}{A L^2}

with I taken from real rolled sections (CHS, RHS, IPE, HEA library) or the solid-square equivalent A^2/12. Members with U>1 are shown red ("FAILS"); the member table, canvas colour mode *Utilisation*, the CSV export and the printable report all carry these values. Caveat: this is an elastic, first-order check — no local buckling, no effective-length factors beyond pin-pin, and no interaction with bending (bars are axial only), so for code work it is a sizing aid, not a substitute for a code check.
